Tropical Cyclones & Ocean Conditions

Tropical cyclones are heat engines that develop when several atmospheric and oceanic conditions align. OceanEmbed's seven surface variables do not replace atmospheric cyclone diagnostics; instead, they provide a window into the ocean surface and upper-ocean environment that can influence cyclone development and intensification.

01

What generally allows a tropical cyclone to form?

The major environmental ingredients identified by tropical-cyclone research.

🌡

Warm Ocean

THERMAL ENERGY

Sea surface temperatures around 26.5°C or higher over a sufficiently deep warm layer are a classic favourable condition for tropical cyclogenesis.

☁

Moist Atmosphere

CONVECTION

Moisture in the middle troposphere supports widespread deep convection. Dry air can disrupt the thunderstorms required by a developing cyclone.

↺

Pre-existing Rotation

VORTICITY + CONVERGENCE

Tropical cyclones generally develop from an existing disturbance containing sufficient low-level rotation and convergence.

⟳

Coriolis Force

SPIN SUPPORT

Sufficient distance from the equator allows the Coriolis effect to support the rotating circulation of the developing system.

⇅

Low Vertical Wind Shear

VERTICAL ORGANISATION

Strong changes in wind speed or direction with height can tilt and disrupt the storm. Lower shear generally favours organisation.

↑

Atmospheric Instability

THUNDERSTORM GROWTH

An atmosphere that supports moist convection allows thunderstorms to efficiently release heat and help the circulation intensify.

Important scientific distinction

The six classic environmental categories above are broader than OceanEmbed's seven surface channels. OceanEmbed directly observes ocean and surface-state information; atmospheric humidity, instability and vertical wind shear are not directly supplied as model inputs.

02

What can OceanEmbed's 7 factors tell us?

Surface signatures that describe the ocean environment around a developing system.

🌡

SST

WARM SURFACE WATER

Higher SST means more available surface heat and evaporation. Sustained warm water is a fundamental oceanic ingredient for cyclone energy.

OceanEmbed: Surface thermal state
🧂

SSS

STRATIFICATION

Freshwater can strengthen upper-ocean stratification and contribute to a barrier layer, affecting how strongly cyclone winds mix surface water with colder water below.

OceanEmbed: Surface salinity state
↕

SSH / SLA

MESOSCALE STRUCTURE

Sea-level anomalies can help reveal mesoscale ocean structures such as warm-core features associated with deeper warm water.

OceanEmbed: Dynamic sea-level structure
↝

U Current

HEAT TRANSPORT

Zonal surface currents redistribute heat and salt and help describe the circulation around fronts and mesoscale features.

OceanEmbed: Zonal surface circulation
↕

V Current

HEAT + WATER-MASS TRANSPORT

Meridional surface currents complement the zonal flow and help identify the transport and organisation of surface water masses.

OceanEmbed: Meridional surface circulation
≋

U Wind

SURFACE FLOW

The zonal wind component describes part of the surface wind field. Its spatial structure can help identify circulation and air-sea momentum exchange.

OceanEmbed: Zonal surface wind
≋

V Wind

CONVERGENCE + FLOW

The meridional wind component complements U Wind. Together, spatial wind patterns can reveal important surface circulation signatures around disturbances.

OceanEmbed: Meridional surface wind
What the U/V wind fields do — and do not — show

High U or V wind values alone do not indicate cyclone formation. OceanEmbed currently uses surface wind components, whereas classical vertical wind shear requires wind information at multiple atmospheric levels.

04

Why the North Indian Ocean?

Regional context matters when interpreting cyclone environments.

Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea

Tropical cyclone genesis in the North Indian Ocean varies strongly with season and location. IMD notes that the Bay of Bengal is more prone to intense systems and that genesis is much less frequent over the western Arabian Sea, partly because of colder SSTs. Pre-monsoon and post-monsoon genesis commonly occurs at lower latitudes.

BoB More favourable for intense systems
AS Strongly seasonal cyclone activity
